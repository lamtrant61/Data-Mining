To investigate the scalability and efficiency of QCAD, we perform a run-
time analysis by varying the sample size, the number of contextual features,
and the number of behavioral features. As shown in Figure 6, we can empir-
ically observe that the runtime of QCAD scales linearly with respect to the
sample size, the number of contextual features, and the number of behavioral
features on small and medium datasets. In contrast, CAD is computationally
prohibitively expensive even on small datasets. To further understand the com-
plexity of QCAD, we perform a time complexity analysis in Appendix A. The
theoretical analysis is in line with our empirical results and analysis.
