Fig. D2 Effects of scaling matched conditional quantile interval length. For each dataset,
the results are obtained by performing 5 independent trials of injecting contextual anomalies.
For each trial, which is represented by the y-axis (namely num data), the results are the
difference (in terms of PRC AUC, ROC AUC, Precision@n, respectively) of methods with
or without scaling the matched conditional quantile interval length. The large differences on
most datasets imply the critical importance of scaling matched conditional quantile interval
length.
As shown in Figure D3, increasing η will lead to higher PRC AUC, ROC
AUC and Precision@n values when η is small (i.e., less than 1 for most
datasets). Next, on the one hand, the ROC AUC stays stable as η increases,
even without clipping (i.e. η = N one). In other words, the clipping strategy
does not affect ROC AUC metric. On the other hand, the PRC AUC and Pre-
cision@n gradually climb to a plateau as η increases. After a certain period,
the PRC AUC and Precision@n start decreasing with an increase of η. Overall,
on most datasets, PRC AUC and Precision@n generally achieve higher values
with 3 ≤ η ≤ 10 than those without clipping. Therefore, the dictator effects
indeed exist and they mainly reduce the performance of QCAD in terms of
PRC AUC and Precision@n. Moreover, our proposed clipping strategy can
effectively overcome this problem.
